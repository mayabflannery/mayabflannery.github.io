## Supervision & Teaching Philosophy

I realize now that a well-defined teaching philosophy is essential to not only being a full-course instructor, but to being an effective TA and mentor as well.

While I am often confident in my own knowledge and abilities in certain subjects, I struggle with coming up with consistent plans and explanations when working with students.
Learning explicitly about the number of teaching strategies was eye-opening.
I could identify some strategies that, as a student, I found to be most effective.
For instance, I have always found 'case studies' to be interesting and memorable when learning new content.
It seems obvious now: case studies are a common teaching strategy and I recognize their use by some of my favourite professors.
But being introduced to this concept as a teaching technique with clear research supporting its use was very helpful.
In writing a teaching philosophy, I can now state that this is a strategy that I believe in and can structure my teaching around.
I also think, since these strategies are clearly outlined, I can consider alternative or complementary strategies in cases where a particular strategy is not effective.

Now that I have a basic teaching philosophy, I am excited to put it into to practice.
I have a lot of flexibility when working with students' independent lab projects.
While I've always had a broad idea of outcomes that a student should achieve with their lab project, I have defined (and plan to further develop) a clear list of outcomes that I can discuss with new students.
I think this will enhance the student's experience, and make me a more effective and efficient mentor.
Related to teaching beliefs, I am committed to continuous improvement.
I like that a teaching philosophy is a 'living document', and I look forward to revisiting it frequently.
I am now motivated to collect feedback from students and explore more literature related to teaching philosophy.

I have enormous respect for good teachers.
Oddly, I have never really thought about what makes them good.
As I continue to think about my own teaching philosophy, I also wonder about the teaching philosophy of my favourite instructors.
What made them so compelling and have such a lasting effect?
